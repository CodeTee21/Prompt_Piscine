# Objective:
   Practice creative writing through prompts.

   Choose a theme (e.g., "friendship," "space exploration," "overcoming fear").

   Write a simple generative prompt: "Write a short story about [theme] in under 300 words."

## prompt:
    "Write a short story about family in under 300 words.

## Model Output:

   The needle touched the old record and made a soft hissing sound. In the Miller family’s house, that sound meant it was time to relax. It was 4:00 PM on a Sunday in Kwara. The weather was hot and humid outside, but inside the house felt calm. Gentle highlife music filled the living room.

   Thomas sat in his favorite armchair with his eyes closed. He tapped his finger slowly on the arm of the chair, keeping time with the music. He looked peaceful after a long week of work. Across the room, Sarah was fixing Maya’s school uniform. She opened the third drawer, took out the scissors, and carefully cut off loose threads. She worked quietly and did not need to ask where anything was.

   Ten-year-old Maya lay on her stomach on the rug. She was drawing their cat in her sketchbook. Her tongue stuck out a little as she focused. The afternoon sunlight shone through the window and brightened her page. The ceiling fan turned slowly above them, moving the warm air around the room.

   Outside, children were playing and laughing in the distance. Cars passed now and then. But inside the house, everything felt still and safe. No one was talking, yet they all felt close to one another. The silence was not empty. It was warm and comfortable.

   When the music stopped, Thomas opened his eyes.

   “Flip it, Maya?” he asked gently.

   Maya stood up carefully and picked up the record by its edges. She placed it back on the player and lowered the needle. The music started again.

   Sarah looked at Thomas and smiled. In that quiet moment, with music playing and simple tasks being done, they felt happy and complete together.

## prompt:
    Write a simple generative prompt: "Write a short story about friendship in under 300 words.

## Model output:

    The orange streetlights showed that another Saturday had ended in Oko Erin. For David and Yusuf, the sound of passing motorbikes was only background noise. They were busy arguing in their usual way.

    They sat on the low concrete wall outside Yusuf’s house. A chessboard rested between them. The pieces did not match. One pawn was a bottle cap, and one knight had a broken ear. Still, the game felt serious to both of them.

    “You are thinking too much,” David said with a laugh. He leaned back against the wall. “The move is simple.”

    Yusuf kept his eyes on the board. “It is simple only if you are not thinking ahead. You think about now. I think about next week.”

    They had been friends since primary school. David liked big ideas and quick decisions. He often talked about starting a business or traveling the world. Yusuf was quieter and more careful. He liked to plan before acting. Together, they balanced each other.

    After a long pause, Yusuf moved the bottle-cap pawn.

    “Check,” he said calmly.

    David sighed, but he was smiling. He did not mind losing to Yusuf. In a world that changed quickly, this wall and this chess game felt steady and safe.

    “Another round?” David asked as he began to arrange the pieces again.

    “Of course,” Yusuf answered.

    They did not need to say they were like brothers. They shared the last of their roasted groundnuts and sat in peaceful silence. Their friendship was not loud or dramatic. It was simple. It was steady. It was always there, night after night.
