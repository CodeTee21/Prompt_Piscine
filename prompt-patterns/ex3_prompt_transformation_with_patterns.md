# Objective: Strengthen weak prompts using patterns.

## Take a vague or under-specified prompt (e.g., "Write about climate change.").

Climate Change

Climate change refers to long-term shifts in temperature, weather patterns, and other environmental conditions on Earth. It is largely caused by human activities, such as burning fossil fuels, deforestation, and industrial processes, which release greenhouse gases like carbon dioxide (CO₂) and methane (CH₄) into the atmosphere. These gases trap heat, causing the planet to warm, a phenomenon known as global warming.

The effects of climate change include rising sea levels, more frequent and severe extreme weather events (like hurricanes, floods, and droughts), melting glaciers, and disruptions to ecosystems and agriculture. Climate change also threatens human health, food security, and biodiversity.

To address climate change, efforts include reducing greenhouse gas emissions, switching to renewable energy, improving energy efficiency, protecting forests, and promoting sustainable practices worldwide. International agreements, like the Paris Agreement, aim to limit global warming and reduce environmental impacts.
    
# Transform it using instructional or comparative patterns:

## Instructional: "Write a 200-word explanation of climate change for high school learners."

Climate Change

Climate change is the long-term alteration of Earth’s climate, including rising temperatures, changing rainfall patterns, and more extreme weather. While the climate naturally changes over time, human activities have greatly sped up the process. Burning fossil fuels like coal, oil, and gas releases greenhouse gases such as carbon dioxide and methane into the atmosphere. These gases trap heat, causing the planet to warm—a phenomenon called global warming.

The effects of climate change are visible worldwide. Ice caps and glaciers are melting, sea levels are rising, and extreme weather events like hurricanes, floods, and droughts are becoming more frequent and severe. These changes also affect ecosystems, leading to habitat loss for animals and changes in plant growth. Humans are impacted too, through threats to food security, health, and access to clean water.

To reduce climate change, we need to cut greenhouse gas emissions and switch to clean, renewable energy sources like solar and wind. Planting trees, protecting forests, and using energy efficiently also help. International agreements, such as the Paris Agreement, encourage countries to work together to limit global warming. Understanding climate change and taking action is essential for protecting our planet and ensuring a sustainable future for everyone.


# Comparative: "Compare the effects of climate change on coastal vs inland regions in 5 bullet points."

1. Rising sea levels: Coastal areas can flood and lose land, but inland areas are not affected by the sea.

2. Storms: Coastal areas get strong hurricanes and big waves; inland areas mostly get heavy rain and floods.

3. Temperature: Inland areas can get very hot or very cold; coastal areas have milder temperatures.

4. Water: Coastal areas may have salty water in wells; inland areas may face droughts and less river water.

5. Nature: Coastal plants and animals (like mangroves and coral) are at risk from sea and ocean changes; inland plants and animals are affected by heat, less rain, and changing seasons.
