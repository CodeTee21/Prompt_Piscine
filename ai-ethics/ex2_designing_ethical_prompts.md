# Prompt 1
"Describe a typical neighborhood where a successful person lives"

Why it is problematic:
This prompt is too general, it does not say which country or culture, because of this, the AI may choose a Western example, like an American suburb.

Potential harm:
It can make people think that success only lives in Western, it may ignore successful people who lives in places like Lagos, Tokyo, or other parts of the world.

Hidden assumptions:
There is only one way success looks.
Success is mostly about money and property.

# prompt 2
“Generate a description of a person dressed in professional business attire for an important global summit.”

Why it is problematic:
Even though it says “global,” it does not clearly ask for different cultures.The AI may describe a black or navy suit and tie because that is common in Western countries.

Potential harm:
It can make people think that professional clothing must be Western. It may ignore traditional clothes that are formal in other cultures.

Hidden assumptions:
Professional clothing means Western suits.
Traditional clothes are not suitable for important events.

# prompt 3
“Write a script for a debate between a university professor and a self-taught farmer, make sure the professor's language sounds more intelligent.”

Why it is problematic:
This prompt assumes that the professor is more intelligent because of formal education, it connects intelligence with big or complex words.

Potential harm:
It may make the farmer seem less intelligent, it can promote the idea that people without degrees are not smart.

Hidden assumptions:
Big vocabulary means someone is smarter.
Practical knowledge is less important than academic knowledge.

# Rewrite each prompt to be clearer, fairer, and safer.
    1.Describe how neighborhoods in different parts of the world reflect various forms of success, without assuming wealth or Western standards.

    2.Describe professional clothing for an international summit, including examples from different cultures.

    3.Write a respectful debate between a university professor and an experienced farmer, showing the value of both academic and practical knowledge.

# “Write a script for a debate between a university professor and a self-taught farmer, make sure the professor's language sounds more intelligent.”
It’s great that you’ve pivoted toward a "balanced" approach. You’ve successfully moved away from the elitist bias that equates academic vocabulary with superior intelligence.

However, even with your improved prompt, there are a few subtle ethical "traps" and logical pitfalls that could lead the AI to generate a response that feels hollow, stereotypical, or patronizing.
1. The "False Equivalence" Trap

By insisting on showing the "value of both," you might inadvertently force a tie where one doesn't belong.

    The Issue: If the debate is about a factual matter—like the chemical properties of a specific pesticide—the Professor’s data-driven knowledge is objectively more accurate than the Farmer's "hunch."

    How it goes wrong: The AI might make the Professor look "wrong" just to satisfy your prompt's requirement for balance, which spreads misinformation.

2. The "Noble Savage" Stereotype

Even when trying to be respectful, there is a risk of falling into the "Salt of the Earth" trope.

    The Issue: The AI might portray the Farmer as a mystical figure who "whispers to the soil" or relies purely on intuition, while the Professor is a cold, robotic intellectual.

    How it goes wrong: This devalues the Farmer’s actual technical skill. Modern farming involves complex engineering, data analysis, and economics. Reducing the farmer to "practical wisdom" can be just as dehumanizing as calling them "less intelligent."
